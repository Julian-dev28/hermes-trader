## What was searched, deliberately, beyond charts

The candle space was already documented as saturated, so this went outside it:

- **funding** (W-FND1) — positioning, not price. A real mechanical cashflow.
- **cross-sectional** (W-XS2) — market-neutral by construction; does not need
  the market to go up, so it fails for different reasons than a directional rule.

Both came back empty on the majors universe.

## Three results worth more than the verdicts

**Cross-sectional momentum expired, it did not fail.** At lookback 120 over 833
days: first half +1.37%, second half **-0.90%**. That is memory's "+1.4%"
xs_momentum measurement and its expiry, visible in one line. The edge was real
and is gone. This is the single strongest argument in the file for why old
measurements must be re-run before they are trusted.

**trendStrength was fit, not found.** The config asserts `+2.08% lift —
strongest edge` on n=497 June trades. On 1140 out-of-sample signals it returns
-0.116% at p=0.149. Those weights still decide which coins the scanner surfaces.

**Funding on majors is asymmetric.** A +2σ funding reading fired 20 times in 208
days; -2σ fired 219. Majors funding is persistently positive with low variance,
so "crowded long" as a z-score event barely exists on this universe. Any future
funding hypothesis here needs an absolute threshold, not a z-score.

## Honest read

Six studies, 39 cells, one validated re-run of something already live. Under the
doctrine the correct response to that is to build nothing, delete what failed,
and record why — not to loosen the bar until something passes.

The bar did its job: three cells looked alive at p<0.05 (W-SESS1 london 0.032,
W-ME2 volumeBuildup1h 0.015, W-XS2 momentum-120 0.094) and all three died on
Bonferroni or on a negative OOS half. Any one of them, reported alone, would
have looked like a discovery.
